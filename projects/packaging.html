When “easy-open” isn’t easy

A product can promise accessibility and still fail the person trying to use it.

Independent research / Food packaging accessibility — A closer look at the problem, the research that shaped the response, and the role I played in moving the work forward.

My role
Lead UX Researcher
Context
Consumer products · Accessibility
Duration
4 months

01 / The challenge

This independent research project examined whether “easy-open” visual cues aligned with the actual experience of opening everyday food packages, particularly when dexterity is reduced.

I designed a within-subjects, mixed-methods study using observed opening tasks, surveys, and Cambridge Simulation Gloves to examine expectations versus reality.

02 / What research revealed

  • Accessibility cues sometimes raised expectations without improving usability.
  • Frustration increased when the promised experience did not match actual effort.
  • Participants judged accessibility through function and predictability, not visual claims alone.
An “easy-open” promise matters only if the opening experience actually delivers.

03 / My contribution

I scoped the research questions, designed the study, collected and analyzed data, and presented findings using both qualitative and quantitative evidence. The analysis explored differences between perceived and experienced accessibility across packaging types.

The work translates usability findings into implications for how products communicate and deliver accessible experiences.

04 / The outcome

The project demonstrated the gap between implied and functional accessibility, and produced findings and recommendations for inclusive packaging design. It was a research study, not a commissioned redesign.
